Student Success & Process Improvement

Identifying challenges, bringing teams together, and using data to improve student experiences, academic performance, and program operations.

Project Overview

As Program Director at Bryan University, I was responsible for overseeing student progress, program performance, and the processes that supported successful online learning experiences.

My approach focused on identifying problems early, understanding what was causing them, and working with the appropriate teams to develop practical solutions.

This involved using Canvas analytics, Power BI reporting, faculty feedback, and collaboration with student support, admissions, and technology teams.

The Challenge

Managing online programs meant addressing challenges that could affect students at different points in their academic journey.

Some students experienced technology problems before starting classes. Others struggled with engagement, coursework, or academic progress after enrollment.

These issues often involved more than one department. Improving outcomes required clearer communication, earlier intervention, and consistent coordination across teams.

Measurable Results

The initiatives I led and supported contributed to improvements in technology readiness, student retention, and academic progress.

~95% Reduction in Technology-Related Late Cancellations
~85% Student Retention Maintained
<3% Students Below Satisfactory Academic Progress Standards

Approximate program results based on operational and student success initiatives. Measures reflect their respective processes and reporting periods.

Improvement Initiatives

The following examples demonstrate how I approached challenges, coordinated solutions, and monitored results.

Initiative 01 | Process Improvement

Improving Technology Readiness

The Problem

Students entering the online design programs needed access to computers and technology capable of supporting their coursework.

Equipment and technology readiness issues were contributing to late cancellations and creating unnecessary barriers before students could begin their programs.

My Approach

I worked with Admissions and IT to improve the process for identifying and addressing technology requirements before students started classes.

The goal was to identify potential problems earlier, improve coordination between departments, and ensure students had the information and support they needed to prepare for their coursework.

Result: Approximately 95% Reduction

The improved technology support process reduced late cancellations related to technology readiness by approximately 95%.

Initiative 02 | Student Retention

Strengthening Early Intervention

The Problem

Students in online programs could begin falling behind before their challenges became obvious through final grades or course outcomes.

Waiting too long to address low engagement or declining academic performance could make it more difficult to help students recover.

My Approach

I monitored student performance and engagement through Canvas, reviewed academic progress, and worked with faculty, coaches, and tutors to identify students who needed additional support.

By coordinating interventions and following student progress, we were able to address concerns earlier and provide more consistent academic support.

Result: Approximately 85% Retention

These coordinated student support practices contributed to maintaining approximately 85% student retention within the programs.

Initiative 03 | Academic Performance

Using Data to Support Academic Progress

The Problem

Supporting student success required a clear understanding of academic performance across the programs, including students at risk of falling below Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP) standards.

My Approach

I used Canvas analytics, Power BI reporting, and academic performance information to monitor trends and identify concerns.

I worked with faculty, coaches, and tutors to coordinate academic interventions and monitor students who needed additional support.

I also used the information gathered through these processes to guide decisions and identify opportunities for improvement.

Result: Below 3% SAP

Through coordinated monitoring and academic support processes, the proportion of enrolled students below SAP standards was maintained at less than 3%.

My Approach to Continuous Improvement

Across these initiatives, I used a consistent process to identify challenges, coordinate solutions, and evaluate progress.

01 / IDENTIFY

Recognize the Problem

Review performance data, student experiences, and operational challenges to identify areas needing attention.

02 / COLLABORATE

Bring Teams Together

Work with faculty and cross-functional departments to understand the issue and develop practical solutions.

03 / IMPLEMENT

Put Solutions in Place

Coordinate changes, clarify responsibilities, and support implementation across the teams involved.

04 / EVALUATE

Monitor the Results

Use data and feedback to measure progress, identify remaining challenges, and guide improvements.

Tools & Technology

Technology supported both day-to-day program management and longer-term improvement efforts.

  • Canvas LMS: Monitoring engagement, course performance, and student progress.
  • Power BI: Reviewing performance information and supporting data-informed decisions.
  • Academic Reporting: Monitoring retention, academic progress, and intervention needs.
  • Cross-Functional Collaboration: Coordinating with faculty, coaches, tutors, Admissions, IT, and other student support teams.

Leadership Impact

These initiatives demonstrate my approach to program leadership: understanding problems, using data to guide decisions, bringing people together, and implementing practical improvements.

Whether addressing student technology readiness, retention, or academic progress, my focus was on improving the processes that supported both student success and program performance.
