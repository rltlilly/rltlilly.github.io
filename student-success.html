01 / Project Overview

Making program operations work better for students.

As Program Director at Bryan University, I was responsible for overseeing student progress, program performance, and the processes that supported successful online learning experiences.

My approach focused on identifying problems early, understanding their causes, and working with the right teams to develop practical solutions.

This involved Canvas analytics, Power BI reporting, faculty feedback, and collaboration with student support, admissions, and technology teams.

Effective program leadership means understanding what is getting in the way and bringing people together to fix it.
02 / The Challenge

Addressing barriers before they grow.

Managing online programs meant addressing challenges at different points in the student experience. These issues often crossed departmental boundaries and required earlier intervention, clearer communication, and coordinated support.

01 / BEFORE ENROLLMENT

Technology Readiness

Some incoming students lacked computers or technology capable of supporting design coursework, contributing to late cancellations.

02 / DURING THE PROGRAM

Student Engagement

Students could begin falling behind in coursework or engagement before challenges were reflected in final grades.

03 / ACADEMIC PERFORMANCE

Academic Progress

Faculty and support teams needed timely information to identify students at risk of falling below academic progress standards.

04 / Improvement Initiatives

The work behind the results.

Three examples of how I identified operational challenges, worked with cross-functional teams, implemented improvements, and monitored outcomes.

Initiative 01 / Process Improvement

Improving Technology Readiness

~95% Reduction in technology-related late cancellations
The Problem

Students entering online design programs needed access to computers and technology capable of supporting their coursework.

Equipment and technology readiness issues contributed to late cancellations and created barriers before students could begin.

My Approach

I worked with Admissions and IT to improve the process for identifying and addressing technology requirements before students started classes.

We focused on identifying problems earlier, improving communication between departments, and helping students prepare for their coursework.

The Result

The improved technology support process reduced technology-related late cancellations by approximately 95%.

Initiative 02 / Student Retention

Strengthening Early Intervention

~85% Student retention maintained
The Problem

Students in online programs could begin falling behind before their challenges became obvious through final grades or course outcomes.

Delayed intervention made it harder for students to recover academically.

My Approach

I monitored performance and engagement through Canvas, reviewed academic progress, and worked with faculty, coaches, and tutors to identify students who needed additional support.

We coordinated interventions and continued monitoring student progress to provide more consistent academic support.

The Result

These coordinated student support practices contributed to maintaining approximately 85% retention in the programs.

Initiative 03 / Academic Performance

Using Data to Support Academic Progress

<3% Students below Satisfactory Academic Progress standards
The Problem

Supporting student success required a clear view of academic performance across the programs, including students at risk of falling below SAP standards.

My Approach

I used Canvas analytics, Power BI reporting, and academic performance data to monitor trends and identify concerns.

I collaborated with faculty, coaches, and tutors to coordinate interventions and follow the progress of students requiring support.

I also used this information to identify opportunities for continued improvement.

The Result

Coordinated monitoring and academic support processes helped maintain the proportion of enrolled students below SAP standards at less than 3%.
